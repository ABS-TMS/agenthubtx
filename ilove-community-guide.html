Why this works

Recognition normally takes months of repeated touches — mailers, postcards, sphere calls. A sticker on someone's window or bumper works differently: it's recognition that compounds passively, every day, without you doing anything after the initial drop-off.

The mechanics matter more than the sticker itself. You deliver it in person — that's the actual relationship moment, the sticker is just the reason to knock on the door. It's free with zero ask attached, which is exactly why people say yes without thinking twice. And you're the named person behind it, not an anonymous directory, so the goodwill accrues to you.

This isn't an untested gimmick, either. "Shop local" sticker and directory campaigns are a well-established pattern — chambers of commerce and city governments run versions of this constantly, and local media outlets like Patch have long given out free window decals tied to a business's directory listing. The sticker-as-goodwill-signal mechanic is proven.

What's different here is the scale it's applied at. Those campaigns are almost always city-wide and run by an institution, not scoped to a single subdivision and fronted by one person. Running it hyperlocal, with you as the visible face instead of an anonymous brand, is the genuinely distinctive part — and it's exactly what makes the goodwill land on you personally instead of evaporating into a logo nobody remembers.

1

Set up your site

Open "My Community Site" from the Tools Directory

Fill out the community name, city, your bio, and your contact info once. Add a photo — upload one from your computer, or paste a link if it's already hosted somewhere.

You'll get three links back immediately: a public directory link, a merchant sign-up link, and an admin link. Save all three somewhere you'll find them again.

2

Get decals and stickers printed

Two versions, one campaign

A window decal for merchants who join the directory, and a bumper sticker for any resident who wants one — both branded "I ♥ [Your Community]." Any local print shop or online sticker printer can produce these cheaply in bulk.

Put your merchant sign-up link and your directory link somewhere visible on the sticker or decal itself, so it keeps working even after you've handed it out.

3

Hand them out yourself

This is the actual relationship-building step

Walk into local businesses and offer a free directory listing plus a window decal — no strings attached. Give residents a bumper sticker the same way, at a community event, door to door, or anywhere you'd normally hand out a business card.

The delivery is the point. A mailed sticker builds nothing; a sticker you hand someone, with your name attached, builds recognition and goodwill in one motion.

Keep it a real no-ask. Don't attach a pitch, a follow-up call, or "let me know if you ever want to sell." The moment it feels like marketing, it stops working the way it's supposed to.
4

Review and approve submissions

Nothing goes live without your review

When a merchant submits themselves through your sign-up link, it lands as pending in your admin page — not live on the directory until you approve it. That keeps the directory spam-free and gives you a natural reason to reach out to every new business personally.

Your admin page also tracks resident sticker requests separately, with a Mark Delivered toggle so you know who still needs a drop-off.

5

Track what the goodwill turns into

Use the Notes field on every listing and request

Every merchant listing and every sticker request has a notes field in your admin page. When someone eventually calls you — a referral, a listing lead, a question — jot it down: "referred by Sarah at the bakery", "became a buyer lead 3 months later."

Without this, the campaign's real return is invisible. With it, after six months you'll actually know whether the stickers are converting into business, not just guess.

Do / Don't

Do

  • Deliver stickers and decals in person
  • Approve listings promptly — a fast "you're live!" builds goodwill on its own
  • Log notes the moment something happens, not months later from memory
  • Keep your bio and photo current — it's the face of the whole site

Don't

  • Attach a sales pitch to the sticker hand-off
  • Auto-approve listings without a quick look — spam ruins trust fast
  • Mail stickers instead of delivering them — you lose the actual moment
  • Let the directory sit stale — an empty "no businesses yet" page undercuts the whole idea

Common Questions

Can I run more than one community site?

Yes — create a separate site for each subdivision you're farming. Each gets its own links, its own directory, and its own set of listings.

What if a merchant asks to be un-listed?

Open your admin page and delete their listing. It's immediate and permanent.

Does the directory cost anything to run?

No — it's included free as part of AgentHubTX. Printing the physical stickers/decals is the only real cost, and that's on you to source locally.

Can someone else see my listings or sticker requests?

No — your admin page only shows data for communities you created. Another agent running their own site can't see yours, and vice versa.
